Results Chart Panel Mock

Second iteration of the mock for feature 006-results-chart-panel: a pie chart in a split pane instead of a bar overlay. Everything inside the terminal below is a character cell, the colours are the app's real theme slots plus a rank-based slice palette drawn from each theme's published family, and every slice combines a colour with a fill texture so the chart survives monochrome. Press g to open or close the pane, or use the buttons. Figures are sample data.

Design notes

Split pane: the watchlist mechanism, reused

The chart opens as a side panel inside the body, exactly like the watchlist panel in src/ui/chrome/frame.ts: panel tone background, a heavy accent rail on its left edge, shown only when there is room (FR-056). The table on the left stays fully interactive – you can keep navigating, sorting and drilling down while the chart follows the selection's context. g toggles it; nothing is modal.

A pie out of character cells

Terminal cells are roughly twice as tall as wide, so the disc is an ellipse in cell space (about 33 × 17 cells here) that reads as a circle on screen. OpenTUI paints per-cell foreground and background, which is all this needs. Edges are chunky by nature; with at most seven slices, every slice stays several cells wide and remains identifiable.

Colour by rank, not by party

Six slice colours per theme, drawn from each theme's own published palette family, plus the muted tone for the aggregate. Colour follows the rank position of the slice, never a party's identity, so no political affiliation is implied (the concern behind the app's original no-party-colours rule). A party's colour can therefore change as rankings change; the adjacent legend is always the authoritative mapping.

Textures keep it monochrome-safe

Each slice pairs its colour with a fill glyph – █ ▓ ▚ ▒ ▞ ░, and · for the aggregate – so slices differ by texture alone in the high-contrast theme, under NO_COLOR and on monochrome terminals. The legend swatch shows the same glyph in the same colour, so the mapping is readable both ways. Try the Vysoký kontrast theme above.

Six slices plus "Ostatní"

The six largest entries get their own slice, ordered clockwise from twelve o'clock; everything beyond the sixth is aggregated into a muted Ostatní slice with the count in its label. That guarantees a legible minimum slice size, and no figure is lost: the legend carries exact votes and share for every slice, and the full breakdown stays in the table on the left.

What the split costs

Half the width leaves about 49 columns for the table, which is less than the full column set needs. While the pane is open the table drops secondary columns from the right – the proportional bar first, then seats – keeping name, votes and share, an extension of the app's existing "lose the aid before any figure" rule. Below the minimum width the palette lists the action greyed with the reason (the FR-069 pattern), and the footer hides the g hint.
